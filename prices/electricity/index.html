Why is the price of electricity (light bill) going up in Nigeria?

Quick answer: Electricity bills rise when tariffs are increased so that they cover the real cost of gas, the exchange rate and running the power network.

The 4 main reasons

  1. Government rules and taxesImport duties, bans, levies and changes to subsidies or tariffs change what sellers pay, and so what you pay.
  2. The exchange rateIt is imported, or made with imported materials or machines. Importers pay in dollars, so when the naira is weaker each dollar costs more naira and the shelf price rises.
  3. World pricesIt is traded around the world. When the world price rises, or a big exporting country sells less, Nigeria pays more too.
  4. The cost of making itFertiliser, seeds, animal feed, packaging and spare parts have become dearer. Producers pass that cost on.

One thing to know

Tariffs are set by the electricity regulator and differ by the hours of supply your area gets.

Will the price of electricity (light bill) come down?

Prices of electricity (light bill) fall when the things that pushed them up ease: government rules and taxes, the exchange rate, world prices. Prices usually rise fast and fall slowly, so slower rises are more common than real falls.
