Why are things so expensive in Nigeria?

Quick answer: Most prices in Nigeria rise for the same few reasons: fuel and transport cost more, the naira buys fewer dollars so imports cost more, farms produce less because of insecurity, weather and storage losses, and power for factories is expensive. The reasons, one by one

  1. Fuel and transportAlmost everything in Nigeria travels by road. When petrol or diesel costs more, every lorry trip from farm or port to market costs more, and that is added to the price.
  2. The exchange rateIt is imported, or made with imported materials or machines. Importers pay in dollars, so when the naira is weaker each dollar costs more naira and the shelf price rises.
  3. The seasonIt is cheapest just after harvest, when markets are full, and dearest months later when stocks run low. This happens every year.
  4. Insecurity in farming areasWhen farmers cannot safely reach their farms, less is planted and harvested. Less supply with the same demand means a higher price.
  5. Losses after harvestA lot spoils on the way to market because of poor storage, bad roads and no cold rooms. Sellers raise the price of what is left to cover what was lost.
  6. Electricity and generatorsFactories, mills, bakeries and cold rooms need power. When they run on diesel or pay more for electricity, the cost goes into the product.
  7. The cost of making itFertiliser, seeds, animal feed, packaging and spare parts have become dearer. Producers pass that cost on.
  8. World pricesIt is traded around the world. When the world price rises, or a big exporting country sells less, Nigeria pays more too.
  9. Festive and school seasonsAround Christmas, Sallah, Easter and school resumption many people buy at once, so sellers can charge more.
  10. Floods and droughtToo much or too little rain damages crops and roads. A bad season in one region raises prices across the country.
  11. Many hands in the middleGoods pass from farmer to collector to transporter to wholesaler to retailer. Each adds a margin and each pays levies on the road.
  12. Government rules and taxesImport duties, bans, levies and changes to subsidies or tariffs change what sellers pay, and so what you pay.
