Why is the price of bread going up in Nigeria?

Quick answer: Bread costs more because wheat flour is imported, and bakeries also pay more for sugar, butter, diesel and packaging.

The 5 main reasons

  1. The exchange rateIt is imported, or made with imported materials or machines. Importers pay in dollars, so when the naira is weaker each dollar costs more naira and the shelf price rises.
  2. World pricesIt is traded around the world. When the world price rises, or a big exporting country sells less, Nigeria pays more too.
  3. Electricity and generatorsFactories, mills, bakeries and cold rooms need power. When they run on diesel or pay more for electricity, the cost goes into the product.
  4. The cost of making itFertiliser, seeds, animal feed, packaging and spare parts have become dearer. Producers pass that cost on.
  5. Fuel and transportAlmost everything in Nigeria travels by road. When petrol or diesel costs more, every lorry trip from farm or port to market costs more, and that is added to the price.

One thing to know

Nigeria imports most of the wheat it uses, so the exchange rate and world wheat prices reach the bread seller quickly.

Questions people ask

How can a family save money on bread?

Switch to a cheaper substitute for some meals. Compare two or three sellers before buying; prices differ even in the same market. Waste less: measure before cooking and store leftovers properly. Yam, sweet potato, plantain, pap or oats can replace bread at breakfast.

Will the price of bread come down?

Prices of bread fall when the things that pushed them up ease: the exchange rate, world prices, electricity and generators. Prices usually rise fast and fall slowly, so slower rises are more common than real falls.
