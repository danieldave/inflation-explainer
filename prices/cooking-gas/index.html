Why is the price of cooking gas going up in Nigeria?

Quick answer: Cooking gas costs more when the world price of gas rises, the naira weakens, or supply to depots is tight.

The 4 main reasons

  1. World pricesIt is traded around the world. When the world price rises, or a big exporting country sells less, Nigeria pays more too.
  2. The exchange rateIt is imported, or made with imported materials or machines. Importers pay in dollars, so when the naira is weaker each dollar costs more naira and the shelf price rises.
  3. Government rules and taxesImport duties, bans, levies and changes to subsidies or tariffs change what sellers pay, and so what you pay.
  4. Fuel and transportAlmost everything in Nigeria travels by road. When petrol or diesel costs more, every lorry trip from farm or port to market costs more, and that is added to the price.

One thing to know

Part of Nigeria's cooking gas is imported, so the exchange rate matters.

Will the price of cooking gas come down?

Prices of cooking gas fall when the things that pushed them up ease: world prices, the exchange rate, government rules and taxes. Prices usually rise fast and fall slowly, so slower rises are more common than real falls.
