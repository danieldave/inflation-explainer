Why is the price of yam going up in Nigeria?

Quick answer: Yam is dearest before the new yam harvest, when old stocks are low, and cheaper after it. Transport and labour costs add to this.

The 4 main reasons

  1. The seasonIt is cheapest just after harvest, when markets are full, and dearest months later when stocks run low. This happens every year.
  2. Fuel and transportAlmost everything in Nigeria travels by road. When petrol or diesel costs more, every lorry trip from farm or port to market costs more, and that is added to the price.
  3. The cost of making itFertiliser, seeds, animal feed, packaging and spare parts have become dearer. Producers pass that cost on.
  4. Losses after harvestA lot spoils on the way to market because of poor storage, bad roads and no cold rooms. Sellers raise the price of what is left to cover what was lost.

One thing to know

Yam is heavy and bulky, so transport is a big part of its price in cities.

What a family can do

  • Buy more when it is in season and store it well.
  • Buy closer to where it is produced when you can: farm-gate and big markets are cheaper than street stalls.
  • Switch to a cheaper substitute for some meals.
  • Cocoyam, sweet potato, plantain and cassava are alternatives.

Questions people ask

How can a family save money on yam?

Buy more when it is in season and store it well. Buy closer to where it is produced when you can: farm-gate and big markets are cheaper than street stalls. Switch to a cheaper substitute for some meals. Cocoyam, sweet potato, plantain and cassava are alternatives.

Will the price of yam come down?

Prices of yam fall when the things that pushed them up ease: the season, fuel and transport, the cost of making it. Because it is seasonal, it usually gets cheaper around harvest.
