Inflation questions and answers

Quick answer: inflation means prices of most things rise over time, so the same naira buys less. In Nigeria the main drivers are fuel and transport costs, the exchange rate, food supply problems and more money chasing the same goods.

What is inflation?

Inflation is when the prices of most things rise over time, so the same money buys less than before. Read more

What causes inflation in Nigeria?

Inflation in Nigeria is mostly caused by higher costs of fuel, transport and imports, a weaker naira, food supply problems, and more money chasing the same goods. Read more

What is Nigeria's inflation rate now?

The National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) publishes Nigeria's official headline and food inflation rates every month in its Consumer Price Index (CPI) report, usually around the 15th. Our home page shows the latest NBS figures; we never make up a rate. Read more

Why is food so expensive in Nigeria?

Food inflation is how fast food prices alone are rising. It matters most to families who spend a large share of their money on food. Food prices go up when transport and fuel cost more, harvests are hit by weather or insecurity, and imported inputs cost more because of the exchange rate. Read more

Why is the price of rice going up in Nigeria?

Rice costs more mainly because of transport and farming costs for local rice, the exchange rate and import rules for foreign rice, and losses from floods and insecurity in rice-growing areas. Read more

What is the exchange rate and why does it change prices?

The exchange rate is how many naira you need to buy one unit of another currency, such as the dollar. When the naira is weaker, everything imported costs more naira. Read more

How do I calculate how much a price has gone up?

Take the new price, subtract the old price, divide by the old price and multiply by 100. For example, if something went from ₦1,000 to ₦1,500, it went up by 500 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 50%. Our price checker does this maths for you. Read more

What is shrinkflation?

Shrinkflation is when the price stays the same but the product gets smaller: a lighter loaf, a thinner bar of soap, fewer biscuits in the pack. Read more

Does inflation eat my savings?

Yes. Money kept in a box or in an account that pays less interest than inflation buys less each year. Read more

How can a family budget when prices keep rising?

List what you spend, put needs before wants, buy in season and in bulk, and review the list every month because prices change. Read more

How do I work out my own inflation for this month?

List the things you buy, how many of each you buy in a month, and the price last month and this month. Multiply each price by how many you buy, add them up for each month, then take (this month − last month) ÷ last month × 100. My Price Diary does this maths for you on your phone. Read more

Who measures inflation in Nigeria?

The National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) measures inflation. It collects thousands of prices across all states each month and publishes the Consumer Price Index. Read more
